\begin{abstract}
This report presents a implementation and empirical analysis of Input-Convex Neural Networks (ICNNs) applied to continuous Optimal Transport (OT) under quadratic cost ($W_2$), reproducing the minimax dual formulation by Makkuva et al. (2020). Using a modular PyTorch-based solver, I validate ICNNs against analytical Gaussian maps, study ablation families across model capacities, investigate failure modes, and evaluate mapping quality against the NeurIPS 2021 Korotin continuous Wasserstein-2 benchmark (\texttt{Mix3ToMix10}) across dimensions $D \in \{2, 4, 8, 16, 32\}$. My results demonstrate that while ICNNs verify 2D transport with high precision (relative $W_2$ error of 0.88\%, L2-UVP of 5.32\%), performance degrades systematically in higher dimensions (L2-UVP reaching 50.80\% at 32D). By capturing iteration-level gradient telemetry, I establish that \textbf{gradient norm explosion and minimax optimization instability}---rather than computational overhead or model parameter constraints---act as the primary bottleneck in high-dimensional optimal transport.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Optimal Transport (OT) defines a mathematical framework for comparing, aligning, and interpolating probability distributions. Computing the Monge map---the map that minimizes the transport cost between continuous distributions---remains a major challenge. Standard deep neural networks fail to enforce the cyclical monotonicity required of optimal mappings. Recently, Input-Convex Neural Networks (ICNNs) have emerged as a mathematically grounded solution. By restricting network weights, ICNNs parameterize convex potentials whose gradients define valid optimal transport mappings.

This report presents a systematic research workflow evaluating the performance, scalability limits, failure points, and benchmark accuracy of ICNN-based optimal transport solvers.

\section{Theoretical Background}

\subsection{Optimal Transport \& Wasserstein Distance}
The Kantorovich formulation of optimal transport between two probability measures $\mu$ and $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}^D$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
W_2^2(\mu, \nu) = \inf_{\pi \in \Pi(\mu, \nu)} \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim \pi} \left[ \|x - y\|^2 \right]
\end{equation}
where $\Pi(\mu, \nu)$ is the set of joint distributions (couplings) whose marginals are $\mu$ and $\nu$.

\subsection{Brenier's Theorem}
For a quadratic cost function, Brenier's Theorem states that if $\mu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, there exists a unique optimal transport map $T: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^D$ pushing forward $\mu$ to $\nu$ ($T_\# \mu = \nu$). Crucially, this map is the gradient of a convex potential $\varphi$:
\begin{equation}
T(x) = \nabla \varphi(x)
\end{equation}
where $\varphi: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}$ is a convex function.

\subsection{Kantorovich Duality}
Using Fenchel-Rockafellar duality, the Wasserstein-2 distance can be rewritten in terms of dual potentials:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{2} W_2^2(\mu, \nu) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mu}[\|x\|^2] + \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \nu}[\|y\|^2] - \inf_{\varphi \in \text{CVX}} \left( \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mu}[\varphi(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \nu}[\varphi^*(y)] \right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Input-Convex Neural Networks (ICNNs)}
To parameterize the convex potential $\varphi$, Amos et al. (2017) introduced Input-Convex Neural Networks. A network $\varphi(x; \theta)$ is convex in its input $x$ if its hidden state updates follow:
\begin{equation}
z_{k+1} = \sigma \left( W_z^{(k)} z_k + W_x^{(k)} x + b_k \right)
\end{equation}
under the constraints that $W_z^{(k)} \geq 0$ for all $k \geq 1$, and $\sigma$ is convex non-decreasing.

\section{Minimax Dual Formulation}
Makkuva et al. (2020) formulate the Fenchel conjugate computation as a minimax game between two ICNNs $f$ and $g$:
\begin{equation}
\min_{f \in \text{CVX}} \max_{g \in \text{CVX}} L(f, g) := \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mu}[f(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \nu} \left[ \langle y, \nabla g(y) \rangle - f(\nabla g(y)) \right]
\end{equation}

\section{Methodology \& Implementation}
A modular PyTorch implementation was constructed under \texttt{src/}. Weights are clipped to non-negative values after each step ($W_z = \max(0, W_z)$). Optimization uses Adam with learning rate $\text{lr}=10^{-3}$ and 10 inner steps on potential $g$ per step of $f$.

\section{Analytical Validation}
Validation on a 2D Gaussian mapping task ($\mu = \mathcal{N}(0, I_2)$ to $\nu = \mathcal{N}([2, -1], 0.6^2 I_2)$) yielded an empirical $W_2 = 2.3270$ against the analytical theoretical $W_2 = 2.3065$, producing a relative error of \textbf{0.88\%}.

\subsection{Key Telemetry Insights}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Gradient Norm Explosion}: $\|\nabla f\|$ increases by $150\times$ from $D=2$ ($0.213$) to $D=32$ ($32.239$).
    \item \textbf{Loss Divergence}: At $D=16$ and $D=32$, $f$-loss increases during early training iterations rather than monotonically decreasing.
    \item \textbf{Constant Compute Cost}: Runtime remains flat ($\sim 135\text{s} - 158\text{s}$), demonstrating that failure is driven by optimization instability rather than computational scaling.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The modular PyTorch solver verifies 2D transport with high precision (0.88\% analytical error) and achieves 5.32\% L2-UVP on the official 2D benchmark. At high dimensions ($D \ge 16$), performance degrades due to gradient norm explosion and minimax optimization instability.
